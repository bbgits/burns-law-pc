# Health Insurance Appeal Letter Template for Illinois Residents

## Overview

You just received a letter from your health insurance company refusing to cover a critical procedure, medication, or hospital stay. The denial letter might claim the treatment is "not medically necessary" or "experimental." It is incredibly stressful, but it is not the final word. If you have been forced to delay care or are facing massive out-of-pocket costs due to a denied claim, you have legal rights - <a href="/insurance" target="_blank">**please contact us ASAP.**</a>.

In Illinois, you have the right to formally appeal an insurance company's decision. Writing a strong appeal letter is your first line of defense. However, a successful appeal is not a complaint about how unfair the situation is—it is a formal, evidence-based legal argument that systematically proves the insurance company made an error. 

The good news? Under both federal law and Illinois statutes, insurers are bound by strict timelines and standards of review. By structuring your appeal correctly, you force them to play by the rules.

## What a Legally Robust Appeal Must Contain

To win an internal appeal, you must directly attack the reason for denial listed on your Explanation of Benefits (EOB). Under the **Illinois Managed Care Reform and Patient Rights Act** (<a href="https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=1265&ChapterID=22" target="_blank">215 ILCS 134/1 et seq.</a>), patients have the right to care consistent with professional standards of medical practice. 

Your appeal letter must contain three critical elements:
1. **Identification of the Error:** Clearly state the claim number, date of service, and the exact language the insurer used to deny the claim.
2. **Clinical Rebuttal:** If the insurer claimed a procedure was "not medically necessary," you must provide clinical evidence proving that it meets the accepted standard of care for your diagnosis.
3. **Statutory & Policy Backing:** Remind the insurer of their obligations. Note that under Illinois law (215 ILCS 134/45), appeals pertaining to clinical matters must be reviewed by a clinical peer—an appropriate health care professional—not just a claims adjuster.

## Gathering the Right Supporting Evidence

Your letter is only as strong as the medical evidence attached to it. Before you send your appeal, gather the following from your treating physician:
* **A Letter of Medical Necessity:** A detailed letter from your doctor explaining exactly why this specific treatment is required for your condition.
* **Relevant Medical Records:** Do not assume the insurance company has your full file. Include all recent lab results, imaging, and clinical progress notes.
* **Peer-Reviewed Literature:** If the insurer called a treatment "experimental," attach articles from respected medical journals proving it is an established, standard-of-care procedure.

## The Illinois Health Insurance Appeal Letter Template

Use the structure below to draft your internal appeal. Replace the bracketed information with your specific details.

**[Your Name]**
[Your Address]
[Your Phone Number]
[Your Email]

**[Date]**

**[Appeals Department Name / Contact Person]**
[Health Insurance Company Name]
[Address from Denial Letter]

**Re: Appeal of Denied Claim for [Patient Name]**
**Policy Number:** [Your Policy Number]
**Claim Number:** [Your Claim Number]
**Date of Service:** [Date]
**Provider Name:** [Your Doctor's Name]

To Whom It May Concern:

I am writing to formally appeal your decision to deny coverage for **[Specific Treatment, Medication, or Procedure]** on **[Date of Denial Letter]**. According to the Explanation of Benefits, this claim was denied because **[quote the exact reason given by the insurer, e.g., "the service is not medically necessary"]**. 

This determination is incorrect. The requested service is entirely medically necessary for the treatment of my diagnosed condition, **[Your Diagnosis]**, and falls within the generally accepted standards of medical care as required by the Illinois Managed Care Reform and Patient Rights Act.

As detailed in the attached Letter of Medical Necessity from Dr. **[Doctor's Last Name]**, this treatment is critical to my ongoing care because **[briefly summarize the doctor's reasoning, e.g., "I have exhausted all preliminary therapies, and my condition continues to deteriorate"]**. 

In support of this appeal, I have enclosed the following documentation:
1. A Letter of Medical Necessity from Dr. **[Doctor's Name]**.
2. My relevant medical records, including **[list specific tests, imaging, or notes]**.
3. **[Optional]** Excerpts from peer-reviewed medical literature demonstrating the efficacy of this treatment.

Under Illinois law, because this appeal pertains to a medical necessity determination, I expect this review to be conducted by a clinical peer who holds the appropriate specialized training in this medical field. 

Please process this appeal within the strict statutory timeframes required by law. Should this internal appeal be denied, I intend to fully exercise my rights under the Health Carrier External Review Act (215 ILCS 180) to request an independent external review through the Illinois Department of Insurance.

I expect a prompt and favorable resolution to this claim. 

Sincerely,

*[Your Signature]*
**[Your Printed Name]**

**Enclosures:** [List the number of pages or items you are attaching]
